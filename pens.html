CHAPTER 02 · THE FOUNTAIN PEN

Self-Contained
Ink

The fountain pen did what quills could not: it carried its ink with it. A rubber sac or cartridge lives inside the barrel, and a slotted feed uses capillary action to feed the nib — relentlessly, gently, blot-free.

  • A reservoir of liquid ink living inside the barrel
  • Slotted feed wicks ink by pure surface tension
  • Breather hole keeps the flow from starving mid-word
  • Flexible steel & 14k/18k gold nibs for handwriting that breathes

CHAPTER 03 · THE BALLPOINT

One Ball To Rule Them All

Journalist László Bíró was tired of smeared ink. With his chemist brother György he married a free-spinning tungsten-carbide ball to quick-drying oil ink — and built the most successful writing instrument ever made.

  • The ball spins 360°, scooping ink and rolling it onto paper
  • Oil ink dries on contact — no blotting, no waiting
  • Roughly 16 billion pens roll off the lines each year
  • One refill draws a line about two kilometres long

CHAPTER 04

The Ink Lab
— Six Formulas

Same tool, six brains · the chemistry of one stroke

Water-Based

Thin, fast-absorbing and gorgeous — the ink of fountains and rollers. Runs like summer, too.

Oil-Based

The ballpoint classic. Dense, greasy, waterproof, eternal — the accountant's quiet ally since 1938.

Gel

Thixotropic paste that flows only when sheared by the ball. Vivid, clicky, doodler-approved.

Pigmented

Fade-proof and water-fast. Blueprints, deeds and museum labels that must outlive us all.

Erasable

Thermosensitive ink goes invisible under friction heat. Mistake management, solved in the 1970s.

Marker

Solvent colour for glass, cardboard and whiteboards. The loudest voice on any desk.
