The Residence

Forty-eight keys carved into a pine forest above the timber line, each one facing a summit that has never, in recorded memory, been climbed twice the same way.

Here, the rituals are quietly Alpine — hairdryers are left in the drawer, pine resin is burned instead of candles, and the only clock that matters is the snow report printed each morning on heavy cream paper.

Backstory

A century in three acts

Act I
1894

The keeper's lodge

A gamekeeper's stone hut and three guest rooms above the timber line. Writing paper, sealing wax, and one rule: “silence after dark.”

Act II
1962

Concrete & cable

The first gondola arrives; the lodge becomes a residence of forty-eight keys, its concrete brushed with local pine and its tariffs printed in francs only.

Act III
2026

Glass & gravity

Today the facade is glass and the roofs hold snow. We still print the snow report by hand at 5 a.m., in heavy cream ink, exactly as 1894 intended.
